# 1. COOKING BASICS

## 1.1 Basic Cooking Techniques

### Sautéing
Cook food quickly in a small amount of fat over medium-high heat. Pan should be hot before adding food. Don't overcrowd — food should sizzle, not steam.

### Boiling
Water at 212°F (100°C) at sea level. Rolling boil for pasta; gentle boil for delicate items.

### Simmering
Small bubbles gently rising to surface (180–200°F / 82–93°C). Best for soups, stews, sauces.

### Braising
Sear food first, then cook slowly in covered pot with small amount of liquid. Low temperature (275–325°F), long time (2–4+ hours). Ideal for tough cuts.

### Roasting
Dry heat in oven, 325–450°F. Higher heat = more browning; lower heat = more even cooking.

### Grilling
Direct heat from below. Preheat thoroughly. Use direct heat for thin cuts, indirect for thick cuts.

### Steaming
Moist heat without submersion. Preserves nutrients and delicate flavors.

### Poaching
Gentle cooking in liquid at 160–180°F. Liquid should show slight movement but no bubbles.

### Deep Frying
Submerge food in oil at 350–375°F. Use thermometer. Don't overcrowd. Drain on wire rack.

## 1.2 Essential Cooking Skills

### Building Flavor Layers
1. Aromatics first — onion, garlic, shallots, ginger, celery
2. Bloom spices in hot oil or fat
3. Deglaze pan with wine, stock, or vinegar to lift fond
4. Finish with acid (lemon, vinegar) and fresh herbs

### Seasoning Throughout
- Salt is the most important seasoning — add at every stage
- Taste as you go
- Acid brightens and balances richness
- Fat carries flavor and adds richness
- Umami (soy, fish sauce, mushrooms, tomatoes, Parmesan) adds depth

## 1.3 Mise en Place

**Definition**: "Everything in its place" — prepare and organize all ingredients before cooking.

### Steps:
1. Read recipe completely
2. Gather all ingredients
3. Wash, peel, chop, measure, portion
4. Organize workspace — tools within arm's reach
5. Preheat oven, pans, grills

### Benefits:
- Prevents mistakes and forgotten ingredients
- Allows focus on technique and timing
- Reduces stress
- Essential for high-heat cooking

## 1.4 Kitchen Workflow

- Left to right: Receive → Prep → Cook → Plate → Serve
- Separate raw protein prep from vegetable prep
- Keep most-used items (spatula, tasting spoon, towel) within reach
- Clean as you go

## 1.5 Advanced Techniques

### Sous Vide
Cook in vacuum-sealed bag in precisely temperature-controlled water bath. Perfect doneness edge-to-edge. Sear after for color.

### Confit
Cook slowly submerged in fat at very low temperature (200–250°F) for hours until tender.

### Deglazing & Pan Sauces
Add liquid to hot pan to dissolve fond. Reduce, mount with cold butter, finish with herbs.

# 3. FOOD SCIENCE

## 3.1 Heat Transfer

**Conduction**: Direct contact (pan to food)
**Convection**: Fluid circulation (oven air, boiling water)
**Radiation**: Electromagnetic waves (broiling, grilling)

## 3.2 Maillard Reaction

Chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars at 140°C+, producing hundreds of flavor compounds and brown color.

**Maximize**: Pat food dry, use heavy pans, don't overcrowd, preheat oil.
**Inhibited by**: Excess moisture, low heat.

## 3.3 Caramelization

Thermal decomposition of sugars alone, above 160°C. Produces sweet, nutty, slightly bitter flavors.

## 3.4 Emulsification

Combining immiscible liquids (oil + water) with emulsifier.

**Emulsifiers**: Lecithin (egg yolk), mustard, honey, xanthan gum.
**Fix broken emulsion**: Start fresh with new emulsifier, whisk broken sauce in slowly.

## 3.5 Fermentation

- **Lactic acid**: Sauerkraut, kimchi, yogurt, sourdough
- **Alcoholic**: Bread, beer, wine
- **Acetic acid**: Vinegar, kombucha

**Optimal**: 68–72°F. Salt critical for vegetable ferments.

## 3.6 Gluten Development

Glutenin + gliadin + water + agitation = gluten network.
**More gluten**: Bread flour, more kneading, more water.
**Less gluten**: Cake flour, less mixing, added fat.
**Windowpane test**: Stretch dough thin — should form translucent membrane.

## 3.7 Protein Coagulation

Proteins denature and bond when heated.
- Eggs: Gentle heat = tender; high heat = rubbery
- Meat: Tightens and squeezes out moisture as temp rises
- Fish: Very delicate — coagulates at lower temps

## 3.8 Sugar Chemistry

- **Crystallization**: Undisturbed saturated solutions form crystals
- **Interfering agents**: Corn syrup, butter, acid prevent crystallization
- **Stages**: Thread → Soft Ball → Firm Ball → Hard Ball → Soft Crack → Hard Crack → Caramel

## 3.9 Fat Behavior

**Smoke points**: Avocado (520°F), Peanut (450°F), EVOO (375°F), Butter (350°F)
**Clarified butter/Ghee**: Milk solids removed. Higher smoke point (450°F+).

## 3.10 Starch Gelatinization

Starch granules absorb water, swell, burst when heated.
- 60°C: Begin absorbing
- 80°C: Swell to 5x volume
- 100°C: Granules burst
- Cooling: Thickens into gel

# 4. INGREDIENT KNOWLEDGE

## 4.1 Vegetables

### Onions
- Flavor: Pungent raw, sweet when cooked
- Storage: Cool, dark, dry place
- Shelf life: 2–3 months whole; 7–10 days cut
- Pro tip: Soak sliced raw onions in ice water to remove harshness

### Tomatoes
- Storage: Room temperature for ripening; refrigerate only when fully ripe
- Best combinations: Basil, mozzarella, garlic, olive oil, balsamic
- Pro tip: San Marzano tomatoes are gold standard for sauce

### Garlic
- Flavor: Pungent raw; mellow, sweet roasted
- Storage: Cool, dark, dry place; 3–6 months whole
- Pro tip: Roast whole heads at 400°F for 45 minutes for sweet, spreadable garlic

### Potatoes
- Types: Starchy (Russet — fluffy, frying/mashing), Waxy (Red — hold shape, salads), All-purpose (Yukon Gold)
- Storage: Cool, dark, dry place; never refrigerate
- Pro tip: Par-boil, rough up edges, roast in hot fat for crispy potatoes

### Carrots
- Storage: Refrigerator crisper; remove greens first
- Best combinations: Ginger, honey, thyme, dill, butter
- Pro tip: Roast at high heat (425°F+) to caramelize sugars

## 4.2 Fruits

### Lemons
- Storage: Room temp for immediate use; refrigerator for longer
- Pro tip: Always zest before juicing; freeze zest and juice in ice cube trays

### Apples
- Types: Eating (Honeycrisp), Baking (Granny Smith), Sauce (McIntosh)
- Storage: Refrigerator crisper; away from ethylene-sensitive produce

### Avocados
- Ripeness: Gently press near stem — should yield slightly
- Storage: Room temp to ripen; refrigerate once ripe
- Pro tip: Keep pit in cut avocado; cover tightly with plastic pressed to surface

## 4.3 Rice

### Types:
- Long-grain (Basmati, Jasmine): Fluffy, separate grains
- Medium-grain (Arborio, Valencia): Creamy — risotto, paella
- Short-grain (Sushi): Sticky texture
- Brown rice: Whole grain, nuttier, longer cooking

### Storage: White rice 4–5 years; brown rice 6–12 months
### Pro tips: Rinse white rice; never stir during cooking (except risotto)

## 4.4 Flour

### Types:
- All-purpose: 10–12% protein — most versatile
- Bread flour: 12–14% — higher gluten for chewy bread
- Cake flour: 7–9% — tender cakes
- 00 flour: Finely milled — ideal for pasta and Neapolitan pizza

### Measuring: Spoon into cup, level with knife. Better: use scale (1 cup AP = 120–125g)

## 4.5 Sugar

- Granulated: Standard white sugar
- Brown: White sugar + molasses (light vs. dark)
- Powdered: Granulated + cornstarch (3% by weight)
- Caster/Superfine: Dissolves faster — meringues, delicate cakes

## 4.6 Salt

- Table salt: Fine, iodized — best for baking precision
- Kosher salt: Coarse, preferred by chefs. Diamond Crystal vs. Morton's differ in density
- Sea salt: Various textures
- Fleur de sel: Hand-harvested, delicate flakes — finishing

## 4.7 Oils

| Oil | Smoke Point | Best For |
|-----|------------|----------|
| Avocado | 520°F | Searing, frying |
| Peanut | 450°F | Stir-frying, deep frying |
| Grapeseed | 420°F | Sautéing, dressings |
| EVOO | 375°F | Dressings, finishing |
| Butter | 350°F | Sautéing, baking |

## 4.8 Butter

- Composition: 80% fat, 16–18% water, 2% milk solids
- European-style: 82–86% fat
- Brown butter: Cook until milk solids turn golden — nutty, complex flavor
- Clarified butter/Ghee: Higher smoke point (450°F+), shelf-stable

## 4.9 Milk & Cream

| Product | Fat Content | Best For |
|---------|------------|----------|
| Whole milk | 3.25% | General cooking, baking |
| Half-and-half | 10–18% | Coffee, light sauces |
| Heavy cream | 36%+ | Whipping, ganache, rich sauces |
| Crème fraîche | 30–40% | Finishing sauces, desserts |

## 4.10 Cheese

- Fresh (Mozzarella, Ricotta, Feta): High moisture, mild
- Soft-ripened (Brie, Camembert): Creamy, edible rind
- Semi-hard (Cheddar, Gouda): Firm, sliceable
- Hard (Parmesan, Pecorino): Low moisture, grateable, intense
- Blue (Gorgonzola, Roquefort): Mold-veined, pungent

## 4.11 Eggs

- Grades: AA (firmest whites), A, B
- Sizes: Recipes assume Large unless specified
- Freshness test: Place in water — fresh sinks horizontal; older tilts or floats
- Fresh eggs best for poaching; older eggs best for hard-boiling (easier to peel)

## 4.12 Chocolate

- Unsweetened: 100% cocoa mass
- Dark: 50–90% cocoa
- Milk: Added milk solids
- White: Cocoa butter + sugar + milk
- Couverture: High cocoa butter (31%+) — ideal for tempering

### Tempering: Heat to 115°F → cool to 80°F → reheat to 88–90°F

## 4.13 Honey

- Composition: 80% sugars, 18% water, 2% minerals/enzymes
- Storage: Indefinite at room temperature. Crystallization is natural
- Pro tip: Measure oil in cup first, then honey — slides right out

## 4.14 Nuts & Seeds

- Almonds: Versatile, mild
- Walnuts: Earthy, slightly bitter — best toasted
- Pecans: Sweet, buttery — pies, pralines
- Pistachios: Vibrant, distinctive — desserts
- Cashews: Creamy, mild — vegan cream sauces
- Pine nuts: Expensive, buttery — pesto. Go rancid quickly

### Storage: Refrigerate or freeze due to high oil content

## 4.15 Beans & Lentils

### Dried Beans:
- Soak: Overnight or quick-soak (boil 2 min, rest 1 hour)
- Cook: Simmer in fresh water 1–2 hours. Salt at end (early salting toughens skins)

### Lentils:
- Red/Yellow: Break down quickly — dal, soups
- Green/Brown: Hold shape — salads, soups
- French (Puy): Peppery — salads

## 4.16 Meat

### Beef Cuts:
| Cut | Best Method |
|-----|-------------|
| Ribeye | Grilling, pan-searing |
| Filet Mignon | Pan-searing, roasting |
| Chuck | Braising, slow cooking |
| Brisket | Smoking, braising |
| Flank/Skirt | Marinate, grill quickly, slice against grain |

### Pork:
- Loin chops: Lean, quick-cooking
- Shoulder (Boston butt): Fatty, tough — pulled pork
- Belly: Rich, layered fat — roast or braise

### Lamb:
- Rack: Quick-roasting
- Leg: Roast whole or butterflied and grilled
- Shoulder: Braised or slow-roasted

## 4.17 Chicken

- Breast: Lean, mild — brine to prevent dryness
- Thighs: Dark meat, more fat, more flavor — hard to overcook
- Brine: 4% salt solution, 30 min–4 hours for juicier results
- Safe at 165°F; can remove at 160°F and allow carryover

## 4.18 Fish & Seafood

### Categories:
- Fatty (salmon, mackerel, tuna): Rich, oily
- Lean white (cod, halibut, tilapia): Delicate, mild
- Medium (snapper, sea bass): Versatile

### Freshness: Clear eyes, bright red gills, firm flesh, clean smell

### Shellfish:
- Shrimp: Cook until pink and opaque (2–3 min per side)
- Scallops: Pat dry, sear for golden crust
- Mussels/Clams: Discard closed before cooking; discard unopened after

# 5. SPICE GUIDE

## 5.1 Flavor Profile Categories

| Profile | Spices |
|---------|--------|
| Sweet | Cinnamon, nutmeg, cardamom, allspice, cloves |
| Savory | Cumin, coriander, turmeric, paprika |
| Spicy/Hot | Chili powder, cayenne, black pepper |
| Earthy | Cumin, turmeric, coriander, fenugreek |
| Smoky | Smoked paprika, chipotle |
| Warm | Cinnamon, cloves, nutmeg, ginger, allspice |
| Floral | Cardamom, saffron, lavender |

## 5.2 Individual Spice Profiles

### Cumin
- Flavor: Earthy, warm, slightly bitter
- Best dishes: Chili, tacos, curries, hummus
- Pairings: Coriander, chili, lime, beans, lamb
- Storage: Whole seeds (1 year); ground (6 months)
- Tip: Toast whole seeds before grinding

### Coriander
- Flavor: Citrusy, slightly sweet, floral
- Best dishes: Curries, sausages, pickling
- Tip: Seed of cilantro plant — completely different flavor

### Paprika
- Types: Sweet, smoked (Spanish), hot (Hungarian)
- Best dishes: Goulash, paella, deviled eggs
- Storage: Refrigerate to maintain color

### Cinnamon
- Types: Ceylon (true cinnamon, milder) vs. Cassia (stronger, more common)
- Best dishes: Baked goods, oatmeal, curries
- Storage: Sticks 2–3 years; ground 6 months

### Turmeric
- Flavor: Earthy, bitter, slightly peppery
- Best dishes: Curries, golden milk, rice
- Tip: Stains everything yellow. Combine with black pepper for absorption

### Cardamom
- Flavor: Complex — citrus, mint, herbal, sweet
- Best dishes: Chai, Scandinavian baking, Indian curries
- Storage: Buy whole pods; ground loses potency within months

### Cloves
- Flavor: Intensely warm, sweet, pungent
- Best dishes: Mulled wine, ham, pumpkin pie
- Tip: Use sparingly — very strong

### Saffron
- Flavor: Floral, honey-like, earthy
- Best dishes: Paella, risotto Milanese, bouillabaisse
- Tip: Most expensive spice. Soak threads in warm liquid 15 min before adding

### Black Pepper
- Flavor: Pungent, sharp, floral
- Tip: Always use freshly ground

### Chili Powder
- Note: American "chili powder" is a blend. Pure ground chilies labeled by type.
- Types: Cayenne (hot), Ancho (mild, fruity), Chipotle (smoky)

## 5.3 Spice Storage

- Airtight containers, away from heat, light, moisture
- Whole spices: 2–4 years; Ground: 6–12 months
- Label with purchase date
- Don't store above stove

## 5.4 Common Mistakes

1. Using old spices
2. Adding spices too late
3. Not toasting whole spices
4. Over-spicing
5. Buying pre-ground when whole available

# 6. HERB GUIDE

## 6.1 Tender Herbs (Add at End)

### Basil
- Flavor: Sweet, peppery, aromatic
- Uses: Pesto, Caprese, tomato sauces, Thai curries
- Storage: Room temp in water; don't refrigerate

### Cilantro
- Flavor: Citrusy, peppery (soapy to some — genetic)
- Uses: Salsa, guacamole, curry, pho
- Tip: Use stems too — concentrated flavor

### Parsley
- Flavor: Clean, grassy, peppery
- Types: Flat-leaf (more flavor) vs. Curly (milder)
- Uses: Gremolata, chimichurri, tabbouleh

### Dill
- Flavor: Bright, grassy, anise-like
- Uses: Fish, pickles, yogurt sauces

### Mint
- Flavor: Cool, sweet, refreshing
- Uses: Salads, lamb, drinks, desserts

### Chives
- Flavor: Mild onion
- Uses: Eggs, potatoes, salads
- Tip: Cut with scissors to prevent bruising

### Tarragon
- Flavor: Sweet, subtle anise
- Uses: Béarnaise, chicken salad, vinaigrettes

## 6.2 Woody Herbs (Add Early)

### Rosemary
- Flavor: Piney, strong, resinous
- Uses: Roasted meats, potatoes, breads
- Tip: Strip leaves from woody stems before chopping

### Thyme
- Flavor: Earthy, lemony, subtle
- Uses: Soups, stews, roasts, stocks
- Tip: Add whole sprigs to braises; remove stems before serving

### Oregano
- Types: Greek (stronger) vs. Italian (milder)
- Uses: Pizza, pasta sauce, Greek salads
- Storage: Dried is more concentrated than fresh

### Sage
- Flavor: Sweet, bitter, earthy
- Uses: Stuffing, brown butter, pork
- Tip: Fried sage leaves are incredible garnish

### Bay Leaf
- Flavor: Herbal, floral, slightly bitter
- Uses: Soups, stews, stocks, braises
- Tip: Always remove before serving

## 6.3 Fresh vs. Dried

| Factor | Fresh | Dried |
|--------|-------|-------|
| When to add | End of cooking or raw | Beginning of cooking |
| Substitution | 1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried | 1 tsp dried = 1 tbsp fresh |
| Best for | Garnishes, salads, bright finishes | Long-cooked dishes, rubs |
| Shelf life | Days to 1 week | 1–3 years |

## 6.4 Storage Summary

- Tender herbs: Stems in water, refrigerate (except basil), cover loosely
- Woody herbs: Wrap in damp paper towel, refrigerate
- Freezing: Chop, place in ice cube trays with oil/water
- Drying: Hang bundles upside down in warm, dry, dark place

# 8. MEAT COOKING

## 8.1 Safe Internal Temperatures

| Food | Safe Temp |
|------|-----------|
| Beef, veal, lamb, pork (steaks, roasts, chops) | 145°F + 3 min rest |
| Ground meat | 160°F |
| Poultry (all) | 165°F |
| Fish | 145°F or opaque & flakes |
| Leftovers | 165°F |

## 8.2 Beef Doneness

| Doneness | Temp | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| Rare | 125°F | Cool red center |
| Medium-Rare | 135°F | Warm red center |
| Medium | 145°F | Warm pink center |
| Medium-Well | 150°F | Small pink center |
| Well-Done | 160°F+ | Little/no pink |

## 8.3 Resting

| Cut | Rest Time |
|-----|-----------|
| Steaks (thin) | 5 minutes |
| Steaks (thick) | 10 minutes |
| Roasts (small) | 10–15 minutes |
| Roasts (large) | 20–30 minutes |

Cover loosely with foil. Remove from heat 5–10°F below target.

## 8.4 Marinating

### Formula:
- Acid (25%): Citrus, vinegar, yogurt, wine
- Oil (50%): Olive oil, sesame oil
- Flavorings (25%): Herbs, spices, garlic, ginger

### Times:
- Fish, seafood: 15–30 minutes
- Chicken breasts: 2–4 hours
- Chicken thighs, pork: 4–12 hours
- Beef, lamb: 4–24 hours

## 8.5 Tenderizing

1. Mechanical: Pounding, scoring
2. Enzymatic: Pineapple, papaya, kiwi, ginger
3. Acidic: Buttermilk, yogurt, vinegar, citrus
4. Aging: Dry-aging concentrates flavor
5. Low and slow: Breaks down collagen

## 8.6 Common Mistakes

1. Not letting meat come to room temperature
2. Overcrowding the pan
3. Moving meat too soon
4. Pressing burgers flat
5. Cutting with the grain
6. Skipping the rest

# 9. BAKING

## 9.1 Bread

### Key Principles:
- Hydration: % of water relative to flour
- Autolyse: Rest flour + water before adding yeast/salt
- Bulk fermentation: First rise — develops flavor
- Proofing: Second rise after shaping
- Oven spring: Rapid rise in first 10 minutes

### Troubleshooting:
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Dense crumb | Under-proofed, too much flour | Extend proofing, measure by weight |
| Flat loaf | Over-proofed, weak gluten | Reduce proofing, strengthen dough |
| Pale crust | Low heat, no steam | Increase temp, add steam |
| Gummy interior | Underbaked, cut too soon | Bake longer, cool completely |

## 9.2 Cakes

### Types:
- Butter cakes: Creamed butter and sugar
- Sponge cakes: Whipped eggs provide structure
- Chiffon: Oil + whipped egg whites
- Angel food: Whipped egg whites + no fat

### Common Problems:
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Collapsed center | Overmixing, too much leavening | Mix gently, measure accurately |
| Dry texture | Overbaked, too much flour | Check doneness early, measure flour correctly |
| Domed top | Oven too hot | Lower temp, use baking strips |
| Tough texture | Overmixed | Mix just until combined |

### Pro Tips:
- Room temperature ingredients emulsify better
- Don't overmix after adding flour
- Test doneness with toothpick — few moist crumbs

## 9.3 Cookies

### Common Problems:
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Spread too much | Butter too soft, too little flour | Chill dough, measure flour |
| Too puffy | Too much flour, too cold | Reduce flour, warm dough slightly |
| Hard/crispy | Overbaked, too much sugar | Reduce bake time |

### Pro Tips:
- Chill dough 30 min to 72 hours
- Underbake slightly — continue cooking on hot pan
- Use room temp butter for creaming; melted for chewier

## 9.4 Biscuits & Scones

- Key: Cold butter/fat. Must stay solid until baking
- Technique: Cut butter into pea-sized pieces. Don't overwork
- Liquid: Buttermilk adds tenderness and rise
- Folding: 4–6 folds create layers

## 9.5 Pastries

### Puff Pastry:
- Lamination: Dough + butter + repeated folding
- 6 turns = 729 layers
- Keep everything cold

### Shortcrust Pastry:
- Ratio: 3:2:1 flour:fat:liquid (by weight)
- Rest 30+ minutes before rolling

### Choux Pastry:
- Boil water + butter, add flour, cook, cool, add eggs
- Bake at 400°F+ for steam puff, then reduce

## 9.6 Pizza Dough

- Hydration: 60–70%
- Cold ferment: 24–72 hours for flavor
- Stretch, don't roll
- Bake at highest possible temp (500°F+)

## 9.7 Professional Tips

1. Weigh ingredients — volume is inconsistent
2. Room temperature matters
3. Use oven thermometer — most ovens run 25–50°F off
4. Rotate pans halfway through
5. Cool in pan 10 minutes before removing

# 10. DESSERTS

## 10.1 Ice Cream

### Base Types:
- French-style: Custard base (egg yolks + cream). Richer, smoother
- Philadelphia-style: No eggs. Lighter, quicker

### Key Principles:
- Higher fat = smoother texture
- Sugar lowers freezing point
- Age base 4–24 hours before churning

## 10.2 Custard

### Types:
- Crème anglaise: Pouring custard
- Pastry cream: Thickened with starch
- Crème brûlée: Baked with caramelized sugar top
- Crème caramel: Baked with caramel sauce

### Technique:
- Cook gently over double boiler
- Temper eggs by slowly adding hot liquid
- Done at 170–175°F — coats back of spoon

## 10.3 Cheesecake

### Keys:
- Cream cheese fully room temperature
- Don't overmix (incorporates air, causes cracks)
- Water bath prevents drying and cracking
- Cool gradually in turned-off oven with door cracked
- Chill at least 4 hours, preferably overnight

## 10.4 Chocolate Desserts

### Tempering:
1. Melt to 115°F
2. Cool to 80°F
3. Reheat to 88–90°F (dark); 86–88°F (milk/white)

### Common Problems:
- Seized: Water got in. Fix with warm cream for ganache
- Bloom: White streaks from temp fluctuations. Still edible

## 10.5 Fruit Desserts

### Cobblers, Crisps, Crumbles:
- Cobbler: Biscuit topping on fruit
- Crisp: Oat + flour + butter streusel
- Crumble: Flour + butter + sugar (British)

### Pie:
- Blind bake: Pre-bake crust before filling
- Thickeners: Cornstarch (clear), flour (cloudier), tapioca (frozen fruit)

# 11. DOUGH

## 11.1 Yeast Dough

### Types of Yeast:
- Active dry: Dissolve in warm water (105–115°F) first
- Instant: Mix directly with dry ingredients
- Fresh (cake): 1 packet dry = 2¼ tsp = 0.6 oz fresh

### Testing Yeast:
- Dissolve in warm water + pinch sugar
- Should foam within 5–10 minutes

### Key Steps:
1. Mixing: Combine until shaggy mass
2. Kneading: 8–10 min by hand, 5–7 min in mixer
3. First rise: Double in volume
4. Punch down: Degas and redistribute
5. Shape: Form desired shape
6. Second rise: Until puffy, 30–60 min
7. Bake: 350–450°F depending on type

## 11.2 No-Yeast Dough

### Soda Bread:
- Baking soda + acid (buttermilk) for leavening
- Mix minimally

### Flatbreads:
- Naan, pita, tortilla, lavash
- Simple flour + water + fat + leavening

## 11.3 Pizza Dough

- Flour: Bread or 00 flour
- Hydration: 60–70%
- Cold ferment: 24–72 hours
- Stretch, don't roll

## 11.4 Bread Dough

- Lean: Flour, water, yeast, salt
- Enriched: Added fat, sugar, eggs
- Whole grain: Requires more water, longer fermentation

## 11.5 Pastry Dough

### Shortcrust (Pie):
- Ratio: 3 flour : 2 fat : 1 liquid (by weight)
- Cold fat, minimal water, don't overwork
- Rest 30+ min before rolling

### Puff Pastry:
- Lamination: Dough + butter + repeated folding
- 6 turns = 729 layers
- Keep cold

### Choux:
- Boil water + butter, add flour, cook, cool, add eggs
- High heat to create steam puff

## 11.6 Cookie Dough

### Types:
- Drop: Soft dough dropped by spoon
- Roll-out: Firm dough rolled and cut
- Refrigerator/Icebox: Dough rolled into logs, sliced
- Pressed: Soft dough forced through press
- Bar: Baked in pan, cut into bars

# 14. RICE

## 14.1 Pilaf (Plov)

### Technique:
1. Sauté aromatics in oil/fat
2. Toast rice in fat until translucent
3. Add hot stock (1.5:1 liquid to rice for white)
4. Bring to boil, cover, reduce to low simmer
5. Don't lift lid or stir
6. Rest 5–10 minutes, fluff with fork

### Uzbek Variations:
- Samarkand: Lighter, with chickpeas and raisins
- Tashkent: Richer, more meat and fat
- Bukhara: Includes quince in autumn
- Fergana: The spiciest

## 14.2 Steamed Rice

- Ratio: 1 cup rice : 1.5 cups water
- Rinse until clear
- Bring to boil, cover, simmer 15 min, rest 10 min

## 14.3 Fried Rice

- Use day-old cold rice
- High heat, small batches
- Order: Aromatics → protein → vegetables → rice → sauce → eggs

## 14.4 Risotto

### Technique:
1. Toast arborio/carnaroli in butter
2. Add wine, stir until absorbed
3. Add hot stock one ladle at a time, stirring constantly
4. Cook 18–20 minutes until al dente
5. **Mantecatura**: Remove from heat, stir in cold butter and Parmesan vigorously

### Key Points:
- Stirring releases starch for creaminess
- Hot stock maintains temperature
- Never rinse risotto rice

# 18. FOOD SAFETY

## 18.1 The Danger Zone

**41°F–135°F**: Bacteria multiply rapidly
- Keep hot foods above 135°F
- Keep cold foods below 41°F

## 18.2 Cross-Contamination Prevention

1. Separate cutting boards for raw meat and produce
2. Wash hands 20 seconds with soap
3. Don't wash raw poultry (spreads bacteria)
4. Store raw meat on bottom shelf
5. Clean and sanitize after raw meat contact

## 18.3 Safe Thawing

1. Refrigerator: 24 hours per 5 pounds
2. Cold water: Change every 30 minutes
3. Microwave: Cook immediately after

**Never thaw at room temperature**

## 18.4 Cooling Hot Foods

- 135°F → 70°F within 2 hours
- 70°F → 41°F within 4 additional hours
- Methods: Shallow containers, ice bath, stirring

## 18.5 Leftovers

- Refrigerate within 2 hours
- Reheat to 165°F internal temp
- Most safe 3–4 days refrigerated

## 18.6 High-Risk Groups

Pregnant women, young children, elderly, immunocompromised should avoid:
- Raw/undercooked meats, eggs, seafood
- Unpasteurized dairy and juices

1. Curl fingers inward, form claw
2. Tuck fingertips, knuckles protruding
3. Rest knife blade against knuckles

# 21. COMMON COOKING PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS

## 21.1 Baking Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Cake collapsed | Overmixing, too much leavening, opening oven early | Mix gently, measure accurately, don't peek |
| Bread didn't rise | Dead yeast, wrong water temp, too much salt | Test yeast, use thermometer, measure salt |
| Bread dense | Under-proofed, too much flour | Extend proofing, measure by weight |
| Cookies spread | Butter too soft, too little flour | Chill dough, measure flour |
| Muffins tough | Overmixed | Mix just until combined |
| Pie crust tough | Overworked, not rested | Handle minimally, chill dough |
| Biscuits not fluffy | Butter melted, overmixed | Keep butter cold, mix minimally |

## 21.2 Sauce Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Sauce separated | Added fat too quickly, too hot | Add slowly while whisking |
| Sauce too salty | Over-seasoned | Add unsalted liquid or cream |
| Sauce too thin | Not enough thickener | Simmer to reduce or add slurry |
| Curdled custard | Overheated | Cook gently, temper eggs |

## 21.3 Meat Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Meat too tough | Wrong method, overcooked lean | Braise tough cuts, don't overcook lean |
| Chicken dry | Overcooked, not brined | Use thermometer, brine |
| Fish falling apart | Overcooked, wrong pan | Use non-stick, cook 70% on one side |
| Burned outside, raw inside | Heat too high | Lower heat, finish in oven |

## 21.4 Other Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Rice too sticky | Too much water, stirred | Use correct ratio, don't stir |
| Rice undercooked | Insufficient liquid | Add ¼ cup hot water, cook 5 more min |
| Soup too salty | Over-seasoned | Add water, acid, or potato |
| Chocolate seized | Water contact | Add warm cream for ganache |
| Cream won't whip | Not cold enough, overwhipped | Use heavy cream, chill bowl |
| Caramel burned | Heat too high | Use medium heat, watch carefully |
| Burned food | Heat too high, not enough fat | Deglaze to save; soak for cleaning |
